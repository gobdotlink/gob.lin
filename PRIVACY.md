# Privacy

gob.lin is designed for local dictation. Microphone access begins only after permission is granted and a dictation is started. Speech recognition runs on the selected on-device engine. Models and tokenizers download directly from their publishers on Hugging Face during preparation, then work offline. With Parakeet v3, Ultra, Redux or Phonon-2, the dictionary correction model downloads the same way after you add your first dictionary term. Download requests disclose your IP address and the requested model files to those hosts; they never include recordings, transcripts, dictionary entries or history. Browsing the bundled catalog makes no network request.

Dictionary suggestions are worked out on your Mac. To suggest terms, gob.lin reads the detected project's package manifests, top-level folder names and the author names in its git history, and the words you fix after a dictation. None of this is stored beyond your dismissals or sent anywhere. A suggested term reaches a Cleanup provider only after you add it, like any other term. Turn off "Suggest words" on the Dictionary page to stop it.

Dictionary entries, settings, and history live on your Mac under gob.lin's Application Support directory. History can include the raw transcript, final text, target app information, timing information, and the original audio. When “Watch edits after paste” is enabled, History also records whether the pasted text was kept, edited, rewritten, or deleted in the following 30 seconds; it stores the edited span so you can see the diff when the edit distance is at most half the dictated length, with a minimum threshold of 10 characters for short dictations. More extensive rewrites are counted but their text is never stored, secure fields and password managers are never watched, and the watch can be turned off in Settings → General. Starred entries are kept when applying retention. You can delete entries and their audio from History. Local history is sensitive: people or software with access to your account may be able to read it.

Cloud rewriting is optional and off by default. When you enable it, gob.lin sends the transcript, chosen tone, cleanup instructions, and preferred dictionary spellings to the provider endpoint you configure. The provider's own privacy and retention terms apply. API keys are saved unencrypted in gob.lin preferences on this Mac, separate from history; other apps running as you can read them. The Apple Intelligence provider instead cleans up with the system on-device model and sends nothing anywhere; disable rewriting, or choose it, to keep transcript processing local. Provider failures preserve the raw transcript and show an error.

Accessibility is used to insert text into the focused field and confirm insertion. Input Monitoring supports global dictation shortcuts. These permissions do not authorize background collection of the contents of unrelated apps. Diagnostics report software versions, permission and device status, timing, and sanitized errors; they must not contain dictation text or API keys. Inspect copied diagnostics before sharing them, since device and application names can identify your setup.

gob.lin does not include analytics or telemetry. No account is needed for local dictation.

The app checks GitHub Releases at launch and hourly for updates to `akalikin/gob.lin`. These requests disclose your IP address and app version to GitHub; they never include recordings, transcripts, dictionary entries, or history. For private releases, the app uses your existing GitHub CLI authentication for github.com, or a token you explicitly save in gob.lin preferences. The token is sent only to GitHub's API, and removed before following asset-download redirects to other hosts. Checks only offer an update; installation and relaunch start when you choose Install. GitHub's privacy terms apply. See [releasing and updating](docs/releases.md).

Development lifecycle events are kept only on this Mac in `~/Library/Logs/gob.lin`, bounded to three 1 MiB files. They include build identity, launch/operation IDs, safe status/error codes, and device tokens that change each launch. They contain no audio, transcripts, clipboard contents, typed keys, credentials, or raw device names/UIDs. Diagnostics can reveal or export this trail; full macOS crash reports are shared separately after inspection. See [collection instructions](docs/diagnostics.md).
