# Privacy

gob.lin is designed for local dictation. Microphone access begins only after permission is granted and a dictation is started, or, with Meetings turned on, when a recording starts (see [Meetings](#meetings)). Speech recognition runs on the selected on-device engine. Models and tokenizers download directly from their publishers on Hugging Face during preparation, then work offline. With Parakeet v3, Ultra, Redux or Phonon-2, the dictionary correction model downloads the same way after you add your first dictionary term, and with Meetings on, the speaker-separation model downloads the first time a meeting is processed. Download requests disclose your IP address and the requested model files to those hosts; they never include recordings, transcripts, dictionary entries or history. Browsing the bundled catalog makes no network request.

Dictionary suggestions are worked out on your Mac. To tell which project you're dictating into, gob.lin reads the window title and page address and looks them up read-only in the local data of T3 Code, Paseo, Codex and Claude, or in Slack's list of signed-in workspaces. To suggest terms, gob.lin reads the detected project's package manifests, top-level folder names and the author names in its git history, and the words you fix after a dictation. None of this is stored beyond your dismissals or sent anywhere. A suggested term reaches a Cleanup provider only after you add it, like any other term. Turn off "Suggest words" on the Dictionary page to stop it.

Dictionary entries, settings, and history live on your Mac under gob.lin's Application Support directory. History can include the raw transcript, final text, target app information, timing information, and the original audio. When “Watch edits after paste” is enabled, History also records whether the pasted text was kept, edited, rewritten, or deleted in the following 30 seconds; it stores the edited span so you can see the diff when the edit distance is at most half the dictated length, with a minimum threshold of 10 characters for short dictations. More extensive rewrites are counted but their text is never stored, secure fields and password managers are never watched, and the watch can be turned off in Settings → General. Starred entries are kept when applying retention. You can delete entries and their audio from History. Local history is sensitive: people or software with access to your account may be able to read it.

iCloud sync is optional and off by default, with two switches in Settings → General. “Sync Dictionary” keeps a copy of your dictionary entries in your own iCloud account. “Sync History” keeps a copy of your History in your own iCloud account's private database: each dictation's raw and final text, the app and project it was for, its timings, tone, model, star and edit details, and the kind and name of the device that made it, such as your Mac's name, along with the time of History's last Delete All. Your other devices signed in to that account share these copies while their switches are on. Recordings, other settings and API keys are never synced, and no gob.lin account or server is involved; Apple's privacy terms apply to the copies. Deleting a dictation deletes it from iCloud and your other devices, while Keep only trims this Mac. Turning a switch off stops syncing and leaves the last copy in iCloud, where you can delete it from your iCloud storage in System Settings. If a different iCloud account signs in, History sync turns itself off and removes other devices' dictations from this Mac; this Mac's own stay.

Cloud rewriting is optional and off by default. When you enable it, gob.lin sends the transcript, chosen tone, cleanup instructions, and preferred dictionary spellings to the provider endpoint you configure. If you also turn on “Send text near the cursor”, it sends the app name, window title and the text near the cursor too: up to 600 characters before it, 200 after it, 600 of any selection, and up to 1,500 characters of other text before the field in its window, such as the messages above a message box. Apple Intelligence never receives this text. The provider's own privacy and retention terms apply. API keys are saved unencrypted in gob.lin preferences on this Mac, separate from history; other apps running as you can read them. The Apple Intelligence provider instead cleans up with the system on-device model and sends nothing anywhere; disable rewriting, or choose it, to keep transcript processing local. Provider failures preserve the raw transcript and show an error.

Accessibility is used to insert text into the focused field and confirm insertion. Before typing a dictation in, gob.lin also reads the window title and the text near the cursor of the field it's about to type into, to fit the dictation to the sentence it lands in. Text in password fields and password managers is never used. With “Send text near the cursor” on, it also reads the text before that field in its window while you talk. In Electron apps such as Slack, gob.lin asks the app to make its text available to Accessibility, as screen readers do. They are used for that dictation only and are not stored; As for every dictation, History keeps the app it was typed into and, when detected, the app and project where it started. Input Monitoring supports global dictation shortcuts. Outside Meetings, these permissions are not used to collect anything in the background from other apps; what Meetings reads, and when, is described below. Diagnostics report software versions, permission and device status, timing, and sanitized errors; they must not contain dictation text or API keys. Inspect copied diagnostics before sharing them, since device and application names can identify your setup.

## Meetings

Meetings is a Labs feature and is off until you turn it on in Settings. While it's off, none of the following runs.

**Spotting a call.** While Meetings is on, gob.lin checks once a second which apps are using the microphone and speakers, through macOS's audio system. It only notes which app and since when; it never hears another app's audio this way. For a known call app (Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Slack, FaceTime, Webex, Discord, or a browser showing a meeting), it reads the app's window titles over Accessibility to find the meeting's name. In a browser, only a title that looks like a meeting (Google Meet, Zoom, Teams and similar) is used, and other tabs are ignored. None of this is kept unless you record.

**What's recorded, and when.** gob.lin records only when you agree to its offer, choose Start, or have set that call app to Always in Settings → Meeting Recording. Recording a call captures two tracks: your microphone, and the call app's own audio through System Audio Recording. In a browser that's the whole browser's sound, so a video playing in another tab is recorded too. Starting without a call app records all system audio. Record Conversation, for people in the room, uses only the microphone. Recording stops when you stop it, shortly after the call app lets go of the mic, or when the Mac sleeps or its lid closes.

**Where it's kept.** Each meeting is a folder on your Mac under `~/Library/Application Support/gob.lin/Meetings`. It holds the two audio tracks, the live transcript, the finished transcript (in JSON and Markdown), the speaker names you give, your marks, when you were muted, the call's participant names, the times you dictated during the call (so those words stay out of the transcript), and a log of the recording's own events. Transcription and speaker separation run on your Mac; nothing in these folders is uploaded. To decide which of your lines were dictations rather than speech to the call, gob.lin compares their times with your local dictation history.

**How long audio is kept.** Audio is deleted one week after a meeting ends. Transcripts, names and marks stay until you delete the meeting. Starring a meeting keeps its audio for as long as it's starred, and a developer setting, `defaults write app.goblin.dictation meetingsKeepAudio -bool YES`, keeps all meeting audio for benchmarking. You can delete a meeting's audio, or the whole meeting, from the Meetings page at any time.

**Reading the call window.** While a call records, gob.lin reads the call app's window over Accessibility about once a second to learn three things: whether you're muted in the call, whether you're sharing your screen, and who's in the call. In a browser it reads only the meeting's page, never the tab strip or toolbar. The window's text is used in memory and then dropped. What's kept is when you muted and unmuted (so what your mic heard while muted stays out of the transcript) and the participant names. Whether you're sharing is never stored; it only hides the Sidekick panel while you share. A hidden debugging setting, `meetingsDebugAccessibilityLog`, keeps the raw window text and a log of every app's microphone and speaker use for test recordings; it's off by default, and with it off that usage log is deleted at launch.

**Speaker-separation model.** Telling the voices apart uses NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Diarization model (about 199 MB) in FluidInference's Core ML conversion. It downloads from Hugging Face the first time a meeting is processed, at a fixed version checked against published checksums, into gob.lin's model folder. Like the speech models, the request discloses your IP address and the files requested, and never includes audio or transcripts.

**Notifications.** gob.lin uses macOS notifications to offer a recording and to say when one is ready or has stopped. They're created on your Mac and can include the call app and meeting name.

**Consent.** Recording laws differ: in some places everyone in a conversation must agree to being recorded. gob.lin reminds you to tell people whenever it offers or starts a recording, but getting their agreement is up to you.

## Other

gob.lin does not include analytics or telemetry. No account is needed for local dictation.

The app checks GitHub Releases at launch and hourly for updates to `gobdotlink/gob.lin`. These requests disclose your IP address and app version to GitHub; they never include recordings, transcripts, dictionary entries, or history. If you are signed in to the GitHub CLI for github.com, or explicitly save a token in gob.lin preferences, the app sends that credential with these requests. The token is sent only to GitHub's API, and removed before following asset-download redirects to other hosts. Checks only offer an update; installation and relaunch start when you choose Install. GitHub's privacy terms apply. See [releasing and updating](docs/releases.md).

Development lifecycle events are kept only on this Mac in `~/Library/Logs/gob.lin`, bounded to three 1 MiB files. They include build identity, launch/operation IDs, safe status/error codes, and device tokens that change each launch. They contain no audio, transcripts, clipboard contents, typed keys, credentials, or raw device names/UIDs. Diagnostics can reveal or export this trail; full macOS crash reports are shared separately after inspection. See [collection instructions](docs/diagnostics.md).
